# [BRAND] — Launch Photography & Video Plan

**Prepared:** 2026-07-08 · **Scope:** 8 hero SKUs · **Customer lens:** quiet luxury, women 25–40 (US/UK/EU)
**Product reality to shoot around:** 18k gold vermeil (warm yellow gold — colour accuracy is critical), IGI-certified lab-grown diamonds (sparkle is the product), made to order (samples are the only inventory — treat them like film props: logged, cleaned, insured).

**Guiding principle:** the macro sparkle shot converts browsers; the on-model shot converts buyers. Mejuri publicly credits on-model *video* as a top conversion driver — every SKU gets a 9:16 loop, no exceptions. Dorsey reshoots its best products 12–20x/year — this plan builds that cadence in from day one instead of treating launch photography as one-and-done.

## 1. Two Production Paths

Run **both**: Path A for the polished ad/PDP engine, Path B for the always-on reshoot cadence and social volume. The DIY kit pays for itself after one photographer session.

### Path A — Local product photographer, half-day (recommended for launch hero assets)

**Budget: $600–$1,500 all-in** (US metro; UK/EU comparable in local currency)

| Line item | Est. cost (USD) |

**Half-day brief — deliverables to put in writing before booking:**

- [ ] **24–40 retouched stills minimum**, agreed per-SKU count (suggested: 5 shots RIV-01, 4 each studs, 3 each remaining SKUs)
- [ ] Every SKU: 1 macro sparkle hero + 1 clean packshot on white (e-comm) + 1 packshot on brand-neutral tone (cream/stone)
- [ ] RIV-01 + STU-01/STU-02: add on-model or on-bust angle if model available
- [ ] 3–5 short **9:16 video clips** (10–20 s each): slow turntable rotation of RIV-01 and one stud, one light-play pass (this is often a cheap add-on — ask)
- [ ] 1 packaging + IGI certificate flat-lay set (3 angles)
- [ ] Delivery: full-res TIFF/PNG **and** web-ready JPEG; 9:16, 4:5, and 1:1 crops of hero shots
- [ ] **Raw files included** (negotiate up-front; some photographers charge extra — worth it)
- [ ] Consistent white balance across the set (send them the cheatsheet in §3)
- [ ] Turnaround date and one round of retouching revisions in writing

**Usage-rights language to request in the contract (copy/paste and adapt):**

> "Client receives a perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive licence to use, reproduce, edit, crop, and distribute all delivered and raw images and video in all media now known or later developed, including but not limited to e-commerce, organic and **paid social advertising**, email, marketplaces, retail/wholesale materials, packaging, and PR, with no additional usage fees, term limits, or territory restrictions. Photographer retains copyright and portfolio-use rights."

Watch-outs: many product photographers quote a low shoot fee but licence "web use only, 12 months" — **paid advertising and perpetuity are the two clauses that matter**. If they insist on a time-limited ad licence, get pricing for the buy-out now, in writing. If a model is involved, a separate signed **model release covering paid advertising** is non-negotiable (template note in §5).

**Video loop specs (all SKUs):** 9:16 vertical, 10–20 s seamless loop, 4K 30/60fps, shot on velvet or on model. Three motion types:
1. **Slow rotation** — turntable or slow manual pan; 1 rotation per 12–15 s, no faster.
2. **Light-play** — product static, move the bare point light in a slow arc so facets fire in sequence. This is the cheapest "expensive-looking" asset you can make.
3. **Put-on motion** — hands entering frame, clasping/pinching/hinging the piece on. The Mejuri-style conversion asset: it sells fit, ease, and realness.

### Reshoot cadence (the Dorsey rule)

Photography is a flywheel, not a launch task. Best-sellers get reshot **12–20x/year** — new backgrounds, new models, new stacks, seasonal light — because ad creative fatigues in 2–6 weeks even when the product doesn't change.

| Cadence | What | Path |
|---|---|---|
| **Every 2 weeks** | 1 DIY mini-session: 2 SKUs, fresh macro + 2 new video loops (rotate through catalogue, RIV-01 + studs get double frequency) | B (DIY) |
| **Monthly** | New on-model set for the current best-seller (UGC creator or F&F session) | B / §5 |
| **Quarterly** | Half-day pro shoot: refresh heroes, shoot any new SKUs, seasonal styling story | A |
| **Trigger-based** | Any SKU whose ad CTR drops >30% from its peak → reshoot within 7 days | B |

## 3. Technical Cheatsheet

### Focus stacking (macro)
- At macro distance, depth of field is ~1–2 mm — a single frame cannot hold a whole stone sharp.
- Lock the phone on the tripod, use the remote clicker, take **5–15 frames**, nudging focus from the front of the stone to the back (manual focus slider in Halide/ProCamera/Lightroom Mobile; some Androids and all recent mirrorless bodies do focus bracketing automatically).
- Merge in Helicon Focus, Affinity Photo, or Photoshop (File → Scripts → Load into Stack → Auto-Blend).
- Nothing may move between frames: putty the product, kill fans/AC, use timer or remote.

### White balance (the vermeil problem)
- 18k gold vermeil reads **orange under warm bulbs** and **greenish-grey under mixed light**. Either one makes the product look plated-cheap or wrong against your PDP swatches.
- Set lights to **5000–5500 K**, both lights the **same** temperature — never mix daylight LEDs with warm room bulbs; turn room lights off.
- Shoot RAW/ProRAW and set custom WB off a grey card in-frame for the first shot of each setup; never trust auto-WB across a set (it drifts shot to shot).
- Sanity check: sterling/white elements in frame (prongs, cert paper) should be neutral white with zero yellow tint; the gold should look warm, not orange.
- Keep one locked WB recipe and reuse it for every reshoot — catalogue consistency is a quiet-luxury signal.

### Sparkle lighting (hard point + diffusion mix)
- **Diamonds sparkle from hard point light; metal looks good under big soft light.** You need both at once:
  - Big soft source (lightbox walls / diffused panel) = smooth gradients on the gold, no blown hot spots.
  - One **bare, small, hard light** (undiffused LED, even a phone torch) raking in at 30–60° = facet fire.
- Move the hard light in tiny arcs while watching the screen; sparkle is angular — a 2° shift turns facets on and off. For video light-play, that slow arc *is* the shot.
- Black velvet base + black cards ("flags") beside the piece deepen facet contrast — diamonds sparkle harder against dark reflections.
- Slightly underexpose (−0.3 to −0.7 EV) to protect highlights; lift shadows in edit. A blown sparkle is unrecoverable.

### Common jewellery photo mistakes (avoid list)
1. **Dust, lint, fingerprints** — invisible to the eye, huge at macro. Gloves on, blower + microfibre before *every* setup; budget retouch time anyway.
2. **Over-diffusing** — an all-softbox setup makes diamonds look like frosted glass. Keep one hard light.
3. **Room reflections in polished metal** — the ring reflects you, the window, the phone. Shoot inside the lightbox, wear dark plain clothing, use white/black cards to control what the metal "sees."
4. **Mixed colour temperatures** — the #1 cause of muddy vermeil. One temperature, room lights off.
5. **No scale reference** — the top cause of jewellery returns/complaints is "smaller than expected." Every SKU needs an on-body or mm-reference shot.
6. **Camera shake at macro** — always tripod + remote/timer; never handheld macro stills.
7. **Blown-out pure-white background swallowing prong detail** — shoot on off-white/grey and lift to white in edit, or expose for the metal and mask the background.
8. **Crooked horizons and inconsistent crops across the catalogue** — set a framing template (product occupies ~70% of frame, consistent margins) and reuse it.
9. **Shooting the sample dirty/scratched** — samples are made to order and precious; log, clean, and re-polish between sessions.
10. **Skipping video** — a static-only catalogue leaves the highest-converting asset class on the table.
