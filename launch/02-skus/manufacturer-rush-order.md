## 2. QC Checklist — Sample Arrival

Run per piece, in order. Photograph every failure before contacting the manufacturer. Do not seed or shoot any piece that fails a hard check.

**Documents first (before opening pieces)**

- [ ] Commercial invoice, packing list, and piece count match the order (19 pcs, correct qty per SKU).
- [ ] XRF plating-thickness report present for every SKU; every reading **≥2.5 microns** and karat stated as 18k. **Hard fail if missing or under-spec.**
- [ ] IGI **finished-jewellery** report present for every piece (not loose-stone certs). **Hard fail** if loose-stone-only.
- [ ] HALO-SP-01: natural centre stone report present; report states NATURAL origin.
- [ ] Verify 2–3 IGI report numbers at random on IGI's online verification portal.

**Per-piece physical checks**

- [ ] **IGI report number vs laser inscription:** under 10x loupe/microscope, confirm girdle inscription matches the report number on inscribed stones. Log SKU → piece ID → report number in a spreadsheet.
- [ ] **Stone count and ctw vs spec:** count every stone (especially RIV-01 — count twice); total ctw per piece within tolerance of spec sheet. No missing, chipped, or loose stones (gently probe pavé with a fingertip / soft point).
- [ ] **Colour/clarity spot check:** stones face-up white and eye-clean; no visibly yellow or included outliers, especially within the Riviere run and between pairs of studs.
- [ ] **Weight check:** gross weight per piece vs manufacturer spec sheet, tolerance ±5%. Log actuals. Underweight can indicate thin castings or under-plating.
- [ ] **Clasp and findings integrity:** lobster clasps spring-tested 10+ times; jump rings soldered closed (not butted); huggie hinges snap positively and stay closed; stud posts straight, notched, butterflies grip firmly; extender chain solid.
- [ ] **Plating and finish under macro lens (or 10x loupe):** even colour with no cloudiness, pitting, bubbling, or bare/thin spots — check high-wear points (clasp, chain ends, hoop hinge, pendant bail, stud post base); crisp prong tips, aligned and seated; no polishing wheel marks, firescale, or plating bleed onto stones.
- [ ] **Stamps:** "925" present and legible; [BRAND] maker's mark correct.
- [ ] **Wear test (one unit of RIV-01 and one stud pair):** wear for a day — check hinge/clasp behaviour, skin comfort, no immediate plating discolouration.

**Disposition**

- [ ] Grade each piece: A = photo/seeding ready · B = usable with caveat · C = reject.
- [ ] Send consolidated QC report with photos to manufacturer within 48 hours of arrival; request written corrective action for anything below A before the production PO.

## 3. Cost-Control Notes

**Sample vs production pricing**

- Expect sample pricing at **1.5–3x the eventual production unit price** — small batches carry setup, handling, and expedite costs. This is normal; do not over-negotiate the sample invoice at the expense of speed or goodwill.
- Ask for sample pricing and indicative production pricing at 50/100/250 units per SKU **on the same quote**, so the sample premium and the volume curve are visible now, not after you're committed.
- Ask whether sample cost (or a portion) is **creditable against the first production PO** — many manufacturers will agree to 50–100% credit; get it in writing.
- IGI finished-jewellery reports typically cost roughly USD 15–40 per piece depending on piece complexity and turnaround — confirm whether certification is included in unit price or billed separately, and whether rush IGI turnaround carries a surcharge.

**CAD/tooling fees to expect**

- CAD design/revision fees: typically USD 25–100 per design; often waived or credited once a production order follows. Confirm you receive and **own the CAD files** (see exclusivity below).
- Tooling/moulds (master models, rubber/silicone moulds): typically USD 50–300 per SKU. Clarify who owns the moulds — you want them owned by [BRAND] or at minimum reserved exclusively for [BRAND].
- Rush surcharge: expect 10–25% on samples for a 10–14 day ex-factory. Acceptable for this round; should not carry into production pricing.

**Negotiate BEFORE the first real PO (leverage is highest now)**

- **MOQs:** push for low per-SKU MOQs (30–50 units) with an aggregate order minimum instead of high per-SKU minimums — you need reorder flexibility while you learn which heroes sell. Ask for lower repeat-order MOQs than first-order MOQs.
- **Defect allowance:** written defect/QC policy — e.g. credit or free replacement for manufacturing defects found within 6–12 months, and an AQL-based inspection standard (defects >2–3% of a shipment triggers rework/credit at manufacturer's cost, including freight on replacements).
- **Design exclusivity on the Riviere (RIV-01):** this is the signature piece — get **written exclusivity**: the manufacturer will not produce this design (or close derivatives) for any other customer, you own the CAD and moulds, ideally worldwide and indefinite, or at minimum 24 months renewed automatically while you meet an agreed annual volume. Extend to the whole collection if the price of doing so is small.
- **Price validity & metal escalation:** fix quoted prices for 6–12 months, with any gold/silver surcharge applying only beyond an agreed band (e.g. ±10% metal price movement) and calculated transparently off a published benchmark.
- **Payment terms:** samples 100% upfront is normal. For production, start at 50% deposit / 50% before dispatch and negotiate toward 30/70 and eventually net terms as trust builds.
- **Plating standard as contract spec:** 2.5 microns minimum with XRF report per batch written into the PO terms — plating thickness is the most common place vermeil suppliers quietly save cost.
- **Lead times with teeth:** agreed production lead time (e.g. 30–45 days) with a small late-delivery credit (e.g. 1–2% per week) — even if rarely enforced, it sets expectations.
- Do **not** commit to a large multi-SKU production PO until this sample round passes QC — the sample pack is your negotiation evidence.
